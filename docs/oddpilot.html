odd-pilot — the campaign copilot, explained

physcheck answers "is this scenario possible?". odd-pilot answers the campaign questions around it: have we tested enough of the real world, what exactly is missing, and can the tool produce those missing tests itself? It never touches a simulator — you keep executing tests however you do today; odd-pilot works on the files and logs around that.

lint→ execute (your simulator)→ assess→ gaps→ plan→ report↻

The idea in one paragraph

Learn from real driving data how often each operating condition occurs — rainy nights, dry motorway afternoons, foggy mornings. Give every combination of conditions a share of real-world operation. A combination counts as sufficiently tested once your executed tests have accumulated enough hours in it — and "enough" is larger for combinations the real world offers more often. When the combinations you have covered add up to almost all of expected operation, testing is adequate and the campaign may stop — with a number you can defend in a safety case. Until then, the uncovered combinations are, precisely, the generator's to-do list.

The three files you need

FileWhat it isFormat
Profiling dataRecords of real operation — fleet logs, drive recordings, even hourly weather observations for your region. One row per observed situation. CSV/XLSX; condition columns named feature_* (categorical — bin continuous values)
Run logWhat you have executed so far. One row per test run, appended as the campaign proceeds (loop appends automatically). CSV; the same feature_* columns + run_duration in seconds
Scenario templateOne OpenSCENARIO file with your road, actors and maneuvers, where the conditions are parameters. plan fills the parameters per generated scenario. .xosc with ParameterDeclarations; feature feature_x fills the parameter named x

odd-pilot model fit — learn how the real world behaves

Fits a Bayesian network over your feature_* columns: not just how often each condition occurs, but how they occur together (rain makes roads wet; night dims the light). The model refuses to save itself if it fails validation.

odd-pilot model fit --data profiling.csv -o world.bn --seed 7
odd-pilot model info world.bn        # nodes, edges, fit quality

Optional: --attribute-map map.yaml binds feature columns to scenario attributes and enables physcheck's L6 realism warnings (lint --model world.bn).

odd-pilot assess — have we tested enough?

odd-pilot assess --model world.bn --log runs.csv --t 2 \
    --alpha 0.05 --rho 0.01 --epsilon 0.05

  t         F     eps   combos  insuff   covered  uncovered  verdict
  2    299.57  0.0500      566     157    0.9606     0.0394  ADEQUATE

Reading the row: of 566 pairs of conditions carrying real-world mass, 157 still lack exposure — but they hold only 3.9 % of expected operation, below the 5 % you allowed (ε). Verdict: ADEQUATE.

The rule, gently. For every combination c of t conditions:

· P̃(c) — its share of real operation (estimated from the model);

· E(c) — hours of your executed tests matching c (from the run log);

· required hours Emin(c) = −ln(α) · P̃(c) / ρ — enough exposure to bound c's undetected event rate below ρ events/hour with confidence 1−α;

· c is sufficient when E(c) ≥ Emin(c); the verdict is ADEQUATE when insufficient combinations hold less than ε of total operation. Adequacy is a coverage claim — failures you observed are triaged separately, never discharged by this number.

Useful extras: --fail-if-inadequate (exit 2 → a CI gate), --sweep eps=0.01:0.10:0.01 (how sensitive is the verdict to ε), --ledger ledger.csv (the full per-combination table), --json adequacy.json (machine-readable, feeds plan and report).

odd-pilot plan — generate the missing tests

odd-pilot assess ... --json adequacy.json
odd-pilot plan --model world.bn -a adequacy.json -k 20 \
    --template templates/junction.xosc -o batches/003/

planned 20 scenario(s) -> batches/003 (3 candidate(s) discarded by the physcheck gate)

For each gap, biggest first: condition the model on that combination and sample candidates — they hit the target condition while everything else stays naturalistic (conditioning on rain gives you wet roads for free). Pick a diverse set, fill your template's parameters, and physcheck every file before it is written into the batch — candidates that violate physics are discarded and replaced. A gap whose every candidate violates physics is reported UNFILLABLE, never silently dropped.

--rarity flips the preference toward rare/extreme variants (criticality campaigns) — with the same physics gate, which matters precisely then. --seed makes batches reproducible; --no-lint disables the gate if you insist. Output: plan.csv (one row per scenario: target, conditions, file) + the ready-to-run .xosc files.

odd-pilot report — the evidence document

odd-pilot lint suite/ --format sarif -o lint.sarif
odd-pilot assess ... --json adequacy.json --ledger ledger.csv
odd-pilot report -a adequacy.json --ledger ledger.csv --lint lint.sarif -o evidence.md

One Markdown artifact for the safety case: the verdict table with an explicit STOP / CONTINUE decision, the adequacy argument written out in ISO 21448 (SOTIF) terms, the scenario-validity summary from the lint, the per-combination exposure ledger, the ranked remaining gaps — and SHA-256 fingerprints of every input, so the document is traceable evidence rather than a pretty printout. --pdf renders it via pandoc where available.

odd-pilot conform — does the suite stay inside your ODD?

# odd.yaml — include/exclude conditions over scenario attributes
odd:
  name: urban-daytime
  attributes:
    env.temperature_k:  {include: {min: 263, max: 313}}
    env.road.wetness:   {exclude: [highFlooded]}

odd-pilot conform suite/ --odd odd.yaml

suite/night_rain.xosc: OUT  (odd 'urban-daytime')
    OUT         env.temperature_k = 250  [Init]

One verdict per scenario — IN, OUT (exercises operation the system is not declared for → exit 1), or UNDECLARED (the ODD constrains something the file does not specify, so conformance cannot be established).

odd-pilot loop — the whole cycle, unattended

odd-pilot loop --model world.bn --log runs.csv \
    --template templates/junction.xosc \
    --exec "./run_carla.sh {scenario}" --until-adequate --max-iter 10

iteration 1: planned=10 executed=10 failed=0 uncovered=0.1841 -> INADEQUATE
iteration 2: planned=10 executed=10 failed=0 uncovered=0.0722 -> INADEQUATE
iteration 3: planned=10 executed=9  failed=1 uncovered=0.0413 -> ADEQUATE
loop finished: adequate after iteration 3

Your executor is any command; {scenario} is replaced per generated file. After each successful run, one row is appended to the run log. The contract, in full:

  • Duration = your command's wall-clock time, unless it prints run_duration=<seconds> on stdout — print the simulated time there when the two differ.
  • A non-zero exit (or --timeout) means the run earns no exposure credit and is reported; if every execution in an iteration fails, the loop aborts rather than burning iterations.
  • Already adequate? The loop exits before touching your simulator. With --until-adequate, the final exit code is the campaign gate: 0 adequate, 2 not.

Choosing the knobs

KnobMeaningGuidance
--tSize of condition combinations (2 = pairs). Start with 2; add 3 when pairs certify. Higher t fragments the mass into many small combinations.
--alpha, --rhoConfidence (1−α) and risk budget (events/hour) behind the required-hours formula. Defaults 0.05 / 0.01. They act only through F = −ln(α)/ρ, so α is a weak lever — set ρ from your safety target and leave α alone.
--epsilonHow much real-world mass may remain insufficiently tested. The well-behaved knob. Use a shrinking per-t schedule — --t 2 3 4 --epsilon 0.05 0.03 0.02 — because mass fragments as t grows.
--n-samplesModel samples behind the mass estimate. Default 50,000 is fine; results are seeded and reproducible with --seed.

Common questions

Does odd-pilot run my simulator? No — never. loop wraps whatever command you give it; everything else works purely on files and logs.
I have no fleet data. Can I still use it? Yes: any tabular record of real operating conditions works — public weather observations for your deployment region are a legitimate start for the environmental part of the model.
What does ADEQUATE actually claim? That the combinations you tested sufficiently carry ≥ 1−ε of expected operation — a coverage claim usable in a SOTIF argument. It is not a claim that no faults exist; observed failures are triaged separately.
Can I use my old research models? Model files from the original PWCC research code load as-is.
Where is the methodology from? The assessment implements the PWCC probability-weighted coverage methodology (paper in submission); the implementation here is cross-validated against the study's reference code — identical verdicts, covered mass within Monte-Carlo tolerance.
