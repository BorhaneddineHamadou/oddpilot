What physcheck finds in public OpenSCENARIO files

Real, reproducible examples: we ran physcheck over the example suites that ship with the two most widely used open scenario engines — CARLA's scenario_runner and esmini — plus ASAM's official ALKS regulatory scenarios. Below, each example shows the exact lines from the public file, the finding physcheck raises, and why it matters.

To be fair to these projects: these are demo/example files, written to exercise engine features — not production test campaigns. They run perfectly well in their engines. That is precisely the point: an engine executes whatever it is given; nothing in the toolchain ever asks whether the declared world is physically possible. These files are what scenario authors copy from — and their placeholder physics silently travels into real campaigns.

CARLA scenario_runner examples

The 200 m/s² template — 20 g of acceleration, copy-pasted everywhere

e.g. ChangingWeather.xosc — the same Performance line appears in most files of the suite (38 findings)

<Performance maxSpeed="69.444" maxAcceleration="200" maxDeceleration="10.0"/>
KIN-018error Vehicle 'hero' declares maxAcceleration = 200.0 m/s²: the fastest production EVs peak at ~1.2–1.3 g (~12–13 m/s²) transiently; street-tire traction sustains ~1 g.
FRI-023error … traction on the declared surface (frictionScaleFactor = 1.0) bounds acceleration to µ·g even for all-wheel drive.

Why it matters: 200 m/s² is a placeholder that became a template. Any test whose outcome depends on the adversary's acceleration envelope (cut-ins, overtakes) is exercised against a vehicle 20× beyond physics — failures and passes alike say nothing about real traffic. The same placeholder value dominates converted datasets too (we found it in all 1,079 SCTrans real-data conversions).

Rain from a cloud-free sky, under a midnight sun

ChangingWeather.xosc, second environment state

<TimeOfDay animation="true" dateTime="2020-01-01T22:00:00"/>
<Weather cloudState="free">
  <Sun intensity="0.05" azimuth="0" elevation="1.31"/>
  <Fog visualRange="100000.0"/>
  <Precipitation precipitationType="rain" intensity="0.9"/>
</Weather>
PRE-012error rain declared with a cloud-free sky: precipitation is by definition a hydrometeor falling from a cloud.
SOL-007warning TimeOfDay = 22:00 with sun elevation 1.31 rad (75°): a sun above the horizon at local night is possible only poleward of the polar circles.
ATM-001warning A Fog element declared with visualRange = 100 000 m — "dense fog with unlimited visibility", the canonical clear-sky idiom that should be an omitted element.

Why it matters: one copy-pasted environment block carries three independent impossibilities. A perception stack "tested in rain" here sees rain streaks lit by a full daytime sun with no clouds — the sensor physics of that world exist in no deployment region.

A pedestrian the size of a car

PedestrianCrossingFront.xosc (same box on the cyclist in CyclistCrossing.xosc)

<Pedestrian model="walker.pedestrian.0001" mass="90.0" ...>
  <BoundingBox>
    <Center x="1.5" y="0.0" z="0.9"/>
    <Dimensions width="2.1" length="4.5" height="1.8"/>
  </BoundingBox>
ENT-010warning Pedestrian bounding box 4.5 × 2.1 × 1.8 m exceeds the adult standing envelope (~0.5 m depth × ~0.6 m shoulder width).

Why it matters: a vehicle bounding box pasted onto a pedestrian. Everything downstream that uses the box — collision verdicts, time-to-collision, "near miss" metrics — is computed against a walker with a car's footprint: crossing tests pass or fail for the wrong reason.

Zero-mass adversary, sprinting at world-class pace

CatalogExample.xosc (entity resolved from its vehicle catalog)

SCH-114error Entity 'adversary' mass = 0.0 kg: mass must be positive.
KIN-002warning Pedestrian 'adversary' reaches 10.0 m/s: only trained sprinters exceed ~8 m/s, and only for tens of seconds.

Why it matters: found through the catalog — physcheck resolves CatalogReferences the way an engine would, so placeholder physics hiding in shared catalogs is caught once, at the source.

Scenarios with no ending

ChangingWeather.xosc, LaneOffsetActionExample.xosc, OscControllerExample.xosc

STB-006warning The storyboard has no StopTrigger condition: the scenario declares no termination and runs until the engine gives up.

Why it matters: in batch campaigns these files rely on external timeouts; their "duration" is an accident of engine configuration — which corrupts any exposure-time bookkeeping built on the logs.

esmini resources

A declared sun 74° away from where the sun could be

cut-in_environment.xosc — caught by the L2 ephemeris against the map's geodetic anchor (37.35° N, −122.09° E)

<TimeOfDay animation="true" dateTime="2025-10-15T12:00:00.012+00:00"/>
<Weather fractionalCloudCover="sixOktas" temperature="278.15" atmosphericPressure="100500">
  <Sun azimuth="2.35" illuminance="80000" elevation="0.4"/>
GEO-005error Declared sun (elevation 22.9° / azimuth 134.6°) is 73.7° away from the ephemeris sun (elevation −27.8°) for 2025-10-15T12:00:00+00:00 at the map origin — 12:00 UTC is 4 a.m. local in California: deep night.

Why it matters: the author meant local noon and wrote +00:00. Shadows, glare and camera exposure in that scene belong to a time that contradicts the file's own clock — exactly the timezone trap that silently skews perception testing. (An adjacent environment in the same file also sets atmospheric pressure to 10 000 Pa — one missing zero, one tenth of sea-level pressure: SCH-109.)

Two vehicles occupying the same point in space

long_dist_action_with_jerk.xosc (also trailers.xosc: tractor and trailers stacked at one pose)

<LanePosition roadId="1" laneId="-1" s="20.0" offset="0.0"/>   <!-- Ego -->
<LanePosition roadId="1" laneId="-1" s="20.0" offset="0.0"/>   <!-- ego jerk -->
MAP-005error Entities 'Ego' and 'ego jerk' interpenetrate at their initial positions ((20.0, −1.5) vs (20.0, −1.5)): rigid bodies cannot overlap at t=0.

Why it matters: engines resolve the overlap by engine-specific magic (spawn nudging, hitch coupling) that is invisible in the file — the same scenario produces different worlds in different simulators.

Driving at 216 % of the road's own speed limit

slow-lead-vehicle.xosc — the limit read from the scenario's own OpenDRIVE map

<AbsoluteTargetSpeed value="30"/>   <!-- road 1 speed record: 50 km/h -->
MAP-007warning 'Ego' is commanded to 30.0 m/s but road '1' has a 13.9 m/s speed limit (216 %). Deliberate speeding should be an explicit scenario parameter, not an accident.

Why it matters: nothing wrong with testing speeding — if you meant to. Undeclared, it shifts every downstream metric (TTC, stopping distance) into a regime the scenario title never admits.

Two speed controllers fighting over one vehicle

parking_demo.xosc — found by the L4 storyboard analysis

STB-003error Event 'Target5UnparkEvent' starts 2 longitudinal actions simultaneously (ReverseActionToEstablishDirection, ReverseAction): they contend for the same control channel on the same actor.

Why it matters: which action wins is an engine implementation detail. The declared behaviour is ambiguous — a portability bug that only shows up when the suite moves to another simulator.

Speed changed at 15 m/s² — beyond any production car

routing-test.xosc

<SpeedActionDynamics dynamicsShape="linear" value="-15.0" dynamicsDimension="rate"/>
KIN-024error Entity 'Ego' commands a speed change at 15.0 m/s²: beyond the ~13 m/s² record launch acceleration and the ~11.8 m/s² braking ceiling.

Why it matters: a demo convenience ("get to the next waypoint fast") that, copied into a braking test, quietly assumes a car that out-brakes physics.

ASAM ALKS scenarios: the control

0 findings across 34 files, at every layer. The regulatory ALKS suite — carefully authored against UN R157 — comes out clean on L0–L5. This is the false-positive control we re-run on every release: a linter that flagged well-authored regulatory scenarios would be measuring its own noise.

And in converted real-data corpora

The same checks over 1,109 scenario files derived from real recorded driving (drone, camera and roadside-sensor datasets) raised zero false alarms about the driving itself — but exposed the conversion pipelines: the CARLA 200 m/s² template pasted into every converted file, 5 × 2 m pedestrian bounding boxes, copy-pasted suns, converted maps that no longer cover their own trajectories, placeholder first trajectory frames that teleport 11 m in 40 ms, and tracker glitches that step speed by 7 m/s in one sample. Full per-finding evidence in the benchmark repository.

Takeaways for practitioners

Violation class you will meetTypical rulesWhat to do
Placeholder performance values (200 m/s², mass 0) travelling via templates and catalogs KIN-018, FRI-023, SCH-114 Lint catalogs and templates first — one fix cleans every derived scenario.
Copy-pasted environment blocks (sun/time/cloud/rain contradictions) PRE-012, SOL-007, GEO-005, ATM-001 Author environments per scenario; declare timezone-aware times deliberately.
Bounding boxes pasted across categoriesENT-010, ENT-011 Keep per-category boxes in the catalog; never copy a vehicle entry for a VRU.
Engine-specific idioms that don't transport (same-pose spawns, competing actions) MAP-005, STB-003 Treat these as portability debt even if your engine tolerates them today.
Undeclared regime shifts (speeding, missing termination)MAP-007, STB-006 Make them explicit parameters so the campaign log means what it says.
